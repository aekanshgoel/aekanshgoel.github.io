Most improvement on a factory floor does not look dramatic. It looks like a clearer standard, a shorter walk, a fixture that makes the next step obvious.

When a process is unstable, people compensate. They remember exceptions, they add extra checks, they keep the line moving through effort. That works until it does not. Continuous improvement is the decision to stop relying on heroics and start making the work easier to do well.

## What I watch for first

- Where operators hesitate
- Where inventory is hiding a timing problem
- Where the written standard and the real work disagree

The point is not to collect waste like trophies. The point is to see the system honestly, change one constraint, and check whether the work got lighter.

> Improve the work so people can do their best without fighting the process.

A question I come back to is simple: `What would make the next unit easier than the last?`
