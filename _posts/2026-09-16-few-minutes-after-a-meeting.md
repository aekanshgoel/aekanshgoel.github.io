The call ends. Someone says thanks. Then everyone sprints.

Slack. Email. The next Zoom. A hallway conversation you were already late for.

I used to treat those next three minutes like leftover time. Dead space. Something to fill.

They are not leftover.

That is when the meeting actually lands. The joke you almost made. The thing nobody said. The one action that matters more than the twelve in the notes.

If you jump too fast, you keep the noise and lose the point.

## What I do now

I stay in the chair. Or I walk. I do not open a new tab yet.

I write down one sentence. Not a recap. One sentence.

What actually happened. What I owe. What felt off.

That is it. Then I go.

On the floor, you do not start the next cycle before you look at the last one. Meetings are the same. The work is not only the talking. It is the second after the talking, when your head is still honest.

Those few minutes are not wasted. They are the cheapest thinking you will get all day.
